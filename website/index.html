Read the Bible. Keep your prayers. Remember the answers.

Bread of Life is a free Bible app that works offline. Your reading plan, prayers and journal stay on your own phone or computer. There is no account, no advertising and nothing tracking you.

Download Source on GitHub

Android, Linux, Windows and macOSVersion 0.4.0MIT licence

In short

  • Works offline

    The Berean Standard Bible, cross-references and Hebrew and Greek word study are built in. Read on a plane or in the bush.

  • No account

    Install it and start reading. There is nothing to sign up for and no email to hand over.

  • No ads, no tracking

    No analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting. The app doesn’t report on you to anyone.

  • Open source

    Every line is on GitHub under the MIT licence. Read it, build it yourself, or change it.

Reading and study

The commentaries sit beside the text

Open the study panel and Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Adam Clarke, John Gill, Keil and Delitzsch and Tyndale follow the chapter you are reading. Tap a verse for its cross-references, or look up the Hebrew or Greek word underneath the English.

Highlight a verse, write a note on it, or send it to your journal or prayer list without leaving the page.

  • TranslationsBerean Standard Bible built in; World English, King James, American Standard and Young’s Literal downloaded when you first open them
  • StudySix public-domain commentaries, 340,000 cross-references, Strong’s Greek and the Hebrew of the Westminster Leningrad Codex
  • MemorisingMemory Lane schedules verses for review with spaced repetition
  • SearchFull-text search across the whole Bible, and across your journal
Listening

Listen while you drive, walk or wash up

Press play on any chapter and the narration carries on through the book and into the next, in the background with the screen off. Play a reading plan and it reads the day’s passages in order, ticking each one off as it finishes.

Spurgeon’s Morning and Evening has a Listen button too. All 732 readings are recorded in a British voice; without a connection, the app reads them with your phone’s own voice.

Android Auto

Bread of Life has its own icon in the car, with tabs for today’s reading, the Bible, the devotional and what you played recently. Say “play John 3” and it does. Chapters you finish in the car count towards your plan.

Prayer

Write down what you asked for, and what happened

Keep a list of the things you are praying about and tap Prayed each time you pray for one. When a prayer is answered, mark it and write down how.

Over months the answered list becomes something you can scroll back through: dates, how long you prayed, and what came of it in your own words. The Faithfulness review lays it out as a single page to print or save as a PDF.

  • RemindersFlag a prayer and it shows on the dashboard each day until you have prayed for it
  • Journal linksLink a prayer to journal entries and verses, in both directions
Plans, devotionals and a journal

A reading plan you can’t fall behind on

Choose from seven plans, from John in three weeks to the whole Bible in a year, or build your own from any range of books. Progress counts the chapters you have read, not the days on a calendar, so a missed week leaves no backlog.

The dashboard gathers the day in one place: the verse of the day, Spurgeon’s morning or evening reading, today’s passages, your streak and your prayers.

  • RemindersAt 2 pm and 8 pm by default. They stop once you have read, even if you read on another device
  • DevotionalsSpurgeon’s Morning and Evening and Faith’s Checkbook
  • JournalFormatted entries with tags, linked to the verses and prayers they are about
Your data

What you write stays on your device

Stored locally

Prayers, journal entries, notes, highlights and reading progress live in a database on your phone or computer. The app works completely without a network.

Sync, if you want it

Sync between devices is optional and off until you turn it on. Use the hosted sync server, or run your own: the server is a small open-source program in the same repository, set up with Docker.

Encrypted before it leaves

Turn on end-to-end encryption and your journal, prayers and notes are encrypted on the device before they sync, so the sync server, hosted or your own, cannot read them. A 24-word recovery phrase unlocks them on your other devices.

What goes online

Only what you ask for: a commentary or translation the first time you open it (then it is cached), audio narration, and the optional AI study helper, which talks to the provider you choose using your own key.

For this device

Android

Install through Obtainium and it updates itself from each GitHub release. No store and no Google account needed. Or download the APK and install it yourself.

Add to Obtainium APK
How to install
  1. Install Obtainium from F-Droid or its GitHub releases.
  2. On your phone, tap Add to Obtainium above. Or in Obtainium choose Add App and paste https://github.com/matt-shearing/bread-of-life.
  3. Obtainium installs the latest APK and checks for new releases.

Android Auto on GrapheneOS needs a one-time setup, described in the mobile notes.

For this device

Linux

An AppImage that runs on most distributions, a .deb for Debian and Ubuntu, and a package in the Arch User Repository.

AppImage .deb
yay -S bread-of-life-bin
Requirements

The app uses the system WebView. Most desktops already have it; on a minimal install add WebKitGTK 4.1 and GTK 3:

  • Debian, Ubuntu: sudo apt install libwebkit2gtk-4.1-0 libgtk-3-0
  • Fedora: sudo dnf install webkit2gtk4.1 gtk3
  • AppImage: chmod +x Bread*.AppImage, then run it.

The AUR package pulls in its own dependencies.

macOS

One universal disk image for Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

Disk image (.dmg)
How to open an unsigned app

The app isn’t signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so macOS blocks it the first time.

  1. Open the .dmg and drag Bread of Life to Applications.
  2. In Applications, Control-click the app, choose Open, then Open again.
  3. On macOS 15 or later, if there is no Open button: try to open the app once, then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway.

If macOS says the app is damaged, run this in Terminal: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Bread of Life.app"
